What this is

A living map of a company that builds itself. It has three views on one engine:

  • Your estate replays the estate's real public records as a growing graph: … public repos as they were created, … couplings, … CI results, Liveware's … receipts, and the pattern library's real nights. Every node and edge is a record you can open.
  • The Living Company plants one seed. fall-core germinates, the organs bud, and every night the pattern library grows through the same gate Kar uses: a rule enters only if it beats a baseline on data it never saw, then passes a second exam. Same seed, same company, every run.
  • Your build takes a process you describe and shows how it would run on these organs. It is always labelled a simulation, because it is one.

It is a re-runnable computation, not an animation: the final structure has a hash, and GitHub's runner recomputes the same hash on every push.

Start here

  1. Press ▶ Play. The estate grows from its first repo to today. Each node appears when that repo was created; each edge appears when both ends exist.
  2. Watch the strip under the graph: every event is also a LIGHT line, the compressed message the organs pass, sent at the same moment its light runs through the organism. Hover a line to read it in plain words and light up the cells it concerns; click it to decode the bytes and check the message is exactly the one sent.
  3. Hover a node to see what it is. In Your estate, the panel links to the repo on GitHub.
  4. Drag the timeline to any moment, or press ↺ Re-run. At the end, the hash chip shows the final structure and the receipt chip says whether it matches what CI recomputed.
  5. Open The Living Company, pick a seed and play. Gold particles that stay are rules the gate kept. Red ones that fade are rules it dropped, and most are dropped. Organs glow brighter as they climb shadow → draft → assist → autonomous.
  6. Open Your build. The example law firm is loaded; press Grow their build, or write your own steps and example cases. Change a step and the hash changes.

How it works

  1. Events, never animation. Each view is a list of events (a repo is built, two repos couple, CI turns green, the gate keeps or drops a rule, an organ climbs a stage), and the kernel applies them one at a time. Anything that cannot be applied to real state is counted and never drawn.
  2. Your estate comes from data/estate.json, written by tools/snapshot.mjs from GitHub's own list of public repos. It refuses to write if GitHub reports any of them private. Repo creation dates order the timeline. Kar's estate map supplies families and couplings. Liveware's receipts, recent CI and the pattern library's nights are added, with any rule that names a private word withheld.
  3. The Living Company runs in a worker in your tab. Each night it samples the public estate, proposes rules (the 12 most precise single words and pairs per family), and runs each through vendor/patlib.mjs, the pattern library's gate, byte for byte. An organ climbs one stage for each kept rule covering its family.
  4. Your build turns your steps into organs. A step that decides something hands its cases to a gate; your example cases go through the same library gate. Every piece is marked a simulation, and the banner stays on screen while it runs.
  5. The LIGHT stream codes each event's message with LIGHT, using vendor/light-codec.mjs primed with a shared dictionary. Clicking a line decodes the bytes and compares them with the message sent. The counter totals real bytes, LIGHT against JSON.
  6. The layout uses FallGarden's own forces (repulsion 3000/d², springs toward 60, a pull to the centre, 0.85 damping), the same constants its graph view uses, in a WebGPU compute shader every frame. At estate scale the renderer adds a speed limit per step and gives repulsion a reach, so 1,700+ nodes stay on screen. Positions are drawing only; they are never part of the hash.
  7. The hash covers the structure (nodes, edges, CI, stages, library), never positions, because a GPU lays out positions in parallel. tools/hashes.mjs recomputes every view's final hash on GitHub's runner and fails the build if any differs from receipts.json.

The living visual: an organism, not a diagram

The graph is drawn as a living thing on top of the same real state. Nothing on it moves for show: every packet, flare, ring, bud and mote is caused by one real event, and the renderer keeps a log of each one with the index of the event that caused it, so it can be checked against the events. A failure (a red CI result, a failed run, a dropped rule) is always drawn red. That is checked by reading the colour back from the graphics card itself, not taken on trust.

Measured on an ordinary laptop's built-in graphics chip, with other work running: every node of Your estate (1,771) at about 64 to 68 frames a second; The Living Company at 70 frames a second or more. Every effect checked against its event: 1,459 of 1,459 in The Living Company, here on this published page. The numbers, the one claim that failed and why, and how each was measured are in RESULTS-living.md.

It breathes

Where every node sits is worked out again every frame on your graphics card (a WebGPU compute shader): the picture settles, then keeps gently moving and never freezes. Two things move without an event, and only these two: the breath, a wave leaving the heart every 6 seconds that swells each cell by up to 6% of its radius, travelling 1,400 world units a second; and the tide, a slow current of 0.06 units a step that turns every 23 seconds, so a region sways together.

Work flows as light

When one part hands work to another, a packet of light travels the vessel between them and rings on arrival. An edge that has carried more work lately (among the last 240 events) carries it faster and brighter: a quiet edge takes 1.6 s, a busy one as little as 0.55 s.

Depth and glow

Every cell sits at a depth: the seed and the conductor in front, the organs just behind, the repos spread over a band. Deeper cells are drawn smaller and nearer the centre in true perspective, and moving your pointer tilts the eye. Light is drawn in high dynamic range and bloomed. The faint dust behind is background, not data.

The library is a mass

The pattern library is a cluster of motes: 96 for every rule the gate kept, up to 4,096 (the gate is strict, so the real libraries here hold one or two rules: the mass is honest, not crowded). Each kept rule's motes fly in from the gate, so the mass densifies; each dropped rule shows one red mote at the rim that fades in 2.4 s. During a night (from Kar handing the day's cases to the gate until that night's last judgement) the mass pulses and turns.

Organs with character

Kar, the conductor, fires routing lines along the vessel as it hands out work. The gate is an aperture that flashes green when it keeps a rule and red when it drops one. The Auditor is a watching eye whose iris widens when the gate judges. The seed's core germinates in rings and swells. A new organ buds out of its parent along a tendril and blooms; it does not pop.

No WebGPU, no fake

A browser without WebGPU (or with ?gpu=0) gets the same real state on a plainer 2D canvas, and the note under the graph says so. There is no video anywhere on this page.

Adoption

For a firm thinking about AI

Describe one process in Your build: its steps, who does each, what it decides, and some real past cases. You watch a model of how it would run before anyone builds anything. The real build then runs on your own hardware, with a signed receipt showing it beats your current way, or you don't pay.

For engineering and assurance

Every view is reproducible. Clone the repo, run node live/tools/hashes.mjs --check, and you get the same hashes as the page and CI. The kernel and its gate are mutation-tested: the test suite has to catch deliberately broken copies of the code, or the build fails.

For procurement and security

The page loads only its own files: its Content-Security-Policy allows nothing else, and it calls no third party. The estate view uses public records only. Your process stays in your browser tab and is never sent anywhere.

White paper: proof you can watch

The problem. AI systems are sold on demos that cannot be re-run, and diagrams that show what a company wishes it had built.

The method. Draw only events. The estate's view replays public records. The grown company is a deterministic computation whose library comes from a real gate. Your build is a simulation that says so on every node. Each view ends in a structure hash that anyone can recompute.

The limits, plainly.

  • The estate view is a replay of a snapshot, not a live feed. The snapshot's date is on the page.
  • Kar's private work and private repos are not shown.
  • The grow view's proposer is a simple word miner, because a visitor's tab has no model. On Kar's machine, the night's 7B also proposes.
  • The gate is strict on this data, so most candidate rules are dropped. That is the gate working.
  • A simulation shows structure and flow, not a delivered system.
